# {metadata.title}

A pile of photographs feels right when it behaves like paper. Prints tuck under each other, the one you reach for comes forward, and the stack slides as a stack.

Getting there was four decisions. Each one I made the obvious way first, and the obvious way was wrong every time.

## Lean belongs to the window, not to the print

Give print `n` a rotation of `n` degrees and the fan looks correct standing still. Then you move it.

<LeanDemo />

The lean is glued to the paper, so the whole row slides as a rigid block. What paper does is roll: a print stands upright as it passes in front of you and leans as it goes away. So the angle has to come from where the print is in the window, not from which print it is.

<CodeBlock
  language="ts"
  code={`
const angle = Math.atan2(dx, Math.max(half, RADIUS));
const lean = clamp(angle * (180 / Math.PI) * (LEAN / 45), -LEAN, LEAN);
const drop = print * Math.min(DROP, SAG * (1 - Math.cos(angle)));
`}
/>

`dx` is the distance from the middle of the window, `half` is half its width. `Math.max(half, RADIUS)` is there for phones: a narrow window means a tight circle, and at 390px the prints leaned into each other hard enough to look like a kink in the row.

CSS does this now, so the server renders the fan and it holds without JavaScript.

<CodeBlock
  language="css"
  code={`
--angle: atan2(var(--dx), var(--r));
--lean: clamp(-10deg, calc(var(--angle) * 0.2222), 10deg);
`}
/>

## Hovering is a state, not a dial

The obvious open is a falloff. The print under your pointer grows most, its neighbours a little less. Every pixel of movement resizes three or four prints and shifts everything after them.

<HoverDemo />

The left row never holds still. The right one moves only when you cross into a different print.

<CodeBlock
  language="ts"
  code={`
const over = printUnder(s, geometry);
if (over < 0) return;          // over a gap: keep whatever is already up
for (let i = 0; i < s.target.length; i++) {
  s.target[i] = i === over ? 1 : 0;
}
`}
/>

That early return is the line I would have left out. Prints have gaps between them, and without it the pile snaps shut every time your pointer crosses one.

## Promoting a layer moves it

Thirteen prints, their shadows and their marks is forty elements holding `will-change: transform`, which is forty compositor layers a phone keeps for something nobody has scrolled to. So I gated it: promote on pointer down, release when the springs settle.

That is the textbook advice and it was the worst bug in the component.

**Giving an element its own layer snaps it to whole device pixels.** The prints sit on fractional ones. So the entire row jumped a pixel or two at the moment of promotion, and I had arranged for that moment to be when your finger touched the screen.

Add the class, change nothing else, diff the pixels:

<CodeBlock
  language="text"
  code={`45,557 pixels changed by more than 2%`}
/>

Promote while the page is already moving instead, and nothing shifts under your hand.

<CodeBlock
  language="ts"
  code={`
new IntersectionObserver(
  (entries) => stack.classList.toggle(ARMED, entries.at(-1)?.isIntersecting ?? false),
  { rootMargin: "200px 0px" },
);
`}
/>

Zero layers before you reach the pile, forty while it is on screen, zero once it is past.

## A frame and the hole it frames must be one element

Each print has a paper border. On a phone it measured like this, with all four sides authored as the same 2px:

<CodeBlock
  language="text"
  code={`
top     12 device px
bottom   4
left     8
right    7
`}
/>

An element that clips and rounds its own corners gets its own rasterised layer, and that layer snaps to device pixels independently of its parent. The border was on the outer box and the photograph in an inset child, so the two rounded apart. Sub-pixel, until a 2.13x lift on a 3x screen multiplies it.

Making the border uniform does not help. The two boxes still snap separately. They have to be one box.

<CodeBlock
  language="css"
  code={`
.photo {
  position: absolute;
  inset: 0;
  box-sizing: border-box;
  border: var(--frame) solid hsl(var(--paper));
  overflow: hidden;
}
`}
/>

Make that border a share of the print rather than a fixed width, too. At a flat 3px it was 2.5% of a desktop print and 4.2% of a phone's, so the same border read half again as heavy on the smaller paper.

## If you hand-roll springs

Semi-implicit Euler is stable only while `k · h² + 2 · c · h < 4`. At my stiffness that is `h < 20.7ms`, and I had capped the frame clock at 32ms so a tab switch could not cause one huge jump.

Below about 48fps the spring diverged instead of settling. Transforms went to infinity, which is not valid CSS and is silently dropped, so nothing looked broken. And a spring that never reaches its target means a loop that never stops.

Sub-steps of 8ms fix it. Check the bound against your worst frame, because a 30Hz display and a laden tab are both past it.

## What I would do differently

Sample every animation frame, not the before and after. I deleted a correct behaviour because a snapshot showed 42 pixels of movement; per frame it was 30 frames with no step larger than 7. That deletion caused the next bug.

And get on a real device early. Emulators caught two of these and missed the two that need real pixel density. The layer jump only became findable when someone tapped the screen and said "it happens on tap".
